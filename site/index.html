The five maps

Each map comes from NASA’s Scientific Visualization Studio. The sound described on each card is planned and does not play yet.

No maps match that search. Clear the search box to see all five.

Precipitation

Rain and frozen precipitation from the Global Precipitation Measurement constellation.

Planned sound Heavier rain becomes a faster pulse. Frozen precipitation is a softer, higher tone.

Latest IMERG composite. NASA precipitation page

Active fires

Fire detections from the VIIRS instrument. Intensity is fire radiative power.

Planned sound Each fire cluster is a beacon. Stronger fires pulse faster, placed left or right by longitude.

Most recent VIIRS detections. NASA active fires page

Carbon monoxide

Near-surface carbon monoxide estimated by NASA’s GEOS-CF model.

Planned sound A low drone thickens where carbon monoxide is higher.

First frame of the animation, not a verified latest observation. NASA carbon monoxide page

Nitrogen oxides

Near-surface nitrogen oxides estimated from nitrogen oxide and nitrogen dioxide.

Planned sound A sharper tone, separate from carbon monoxide, marks higher nitrogen oxides.

First frame of the animation, not a verified latest observation. NASA nitrogen oxides page

Temperature

Air temperature about 3 meters above the surface, from NASA’s GEOS-FP weather analysis.

Planned sound Pitch follows the temperature. Colder air is lower. Hotter air is higher, on a musical scale.

First frame of the animation, not a verified latest observation. NASA temperature page

Navigation

How to navigate

Buttons, keys, and voice commands all do the same things. Use whichever works best for you.

Keyboard

  • Tab Move to the next link, button, or search box.
  • Enter Follow a link or press a button.
  • Esc Close the menu or the page overview.

Previous section and Next section move to the next heading and announce where you landed.

Voice commands

Press Start voice, then say one of these. You can also say rain, fires, heat, photo of the day, or read the overview.

    For everyone

    Accessibility

    This site is built to work for people who are blind or have low vision, people who cannot read color maps, and anyone using a keyboard or their voice instead of a mouse.

    • Text and buttons meet the enhanced contrast level, at least 7 to 1.
    • Every control works from the keyboard and shows a clear focus outline.
    • Links in text are underlined. Meaning is never shown by color alone.
    • Each map has a written description of what the picture shows, plus its source.
    • Text can be enlarged, and the layout reflows on narrow screens and at high zoom.
    • Search results and section changes are announced to screen readers.
    • The microphone turns on only after you press Start voice. Your speech is recognized on this computer by the speech engine built into Windows. It is not sent to an online service.
    • Nothing moves or plays on its own.

    Found a barrier? Report an accessibility problem.

    Page overview

    Earth Information Jukebox. A NASA Space Apps project, not the official NASA website.

    This page presents five NASA Earth maps: precipitation, active fires, carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, and near-surface temperature. It also has a space photo of the day. Precipitation and fires are NASA’s latest published images. The other three are the first frame of an animation, not a verified latest observation.

    You can search the maps, read what each one shows, and move with Previous and Next. Start voice, then say next, previous, photo, a map name, or page overview. Say stop speaking to quiet the voice, or stop listening to turn the microphone off.
