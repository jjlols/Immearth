Navigation

How to navigate

Buttons, keys, and voice commands all do the same things. Use whichever works best for you.

Keyboard

  • Tab Move to the next link, button, or search box.
  • Enter Follow a link or press a button.
  • Esc Close the menu or the page overview, and stop test tones if they are playing.
  • ← → Turn while the Listening direction slider is focused. Arrow keys do not turn from the rest of the page.

Previous section and Next section move to the next heading and announce where you landed.

Voice commands

Press Start voice, then say one of these. You can also say rain, fires, heat, photo of the day, read the overview, turn left, turn right, recenter, or test tones.

    For everyone

    Accessibility

    This site is built to work for people who are blind or have low vision, people who cannot read color maps, and anyone using a keyboard or their voice instead of a mouse.

    • Text and buttons meet the enhanced contrast level, at least 7 to 1.
    • Every control works from the keyboard and shows a clear focus outline.
    • Links in text are underlined. Meaning is never shown by color alone.
    • Each map has a written description of what the picture shows, plus its source.
    • Text can be enlarged, and the layout reflows on narrow screens and at high zoom.
    • Search results and section changes are announced to screen readers.
    • The microphone turns on only after you press Start voice. Your speech is recognized on this computer by the speech engine built into Windows. It is not sent to an online service.
    • Immersive listening stays quiet until you press Play test tones or another sound is started. A text line shows which way you are facing. Headphones are recommended. The camera stays off until you ask, and the picture is processed on this computer.
    • An opening animation plays when you arrive. Press Space, Escape, or Skip, or click the animation, and it fades away. It stays off when your device is set to reduce motion.

    Page overview

    Earth Information Jukebox. A NASA Space Apps project, not the official NASA website.

    This page presents five NASA Earth maps: precipitation, active fires, carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, and near-surface temperature. It also has a space photo of the day. Precipitation and fires are NASA’s latest published images. The other three are the first frame of an animation, not a verified latest observation.

    You can search the maps, read what each one shows, and move with Previous and Next. Start voice, then say next, previous, photo, a map name, or page overview. Say stop speaking to quiet the voice, or stop listening to turn the microphone off.

    Immersive listening can place sound around you. Headphones are recommended. Use the direction slider, or say turn left, turn right, recenter, or test tones. The camera stays off unless you turn it on, and that picture stays on this computer.
